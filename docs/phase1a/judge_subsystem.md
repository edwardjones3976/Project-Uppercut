# The Judge subsystem: Rule 3 as a module boundary

**Date:** 2026-08-04 · **Decision 0012 slice item 1** · next_steps Step B, first half

Minimum scope has named "a deterministic Judge subsystem behind the score
firewall" since Phase 0, and the 2026-08-03 next-steps review said precisely
what existed instead: `Bout._score_round`, a ten-point-must computed inline on
the same object that resolves contact, reading `self._cards` directly — Rule 3
holding as a property of one method's return type. This records the extraction
that turned it into a subsystem with a boundary a validator checks.

## What exists now

`tools/uppercut_sim/judge.py` — the Judge. Observation stream in, sealed cards
out, and both halves of that sentence are mechanical:

- **In:** the engine shows the judge *witnessed facts* — plain dicts naming a
  punch landed (scorer, delivered force) or a knockdown (who went down).
  Nothing else crosses. The judge imports nothing, so it cannot even name
  `Fighter`, `Bout`, canon, policy, or the RNG, let alone read them.
- **Out:** sealed cards. `sealed_cards()` before `seal_final()` raises
  `SealedCardsError`; so does witnessing a fact after the official reading.
  `Bout.decision()` — the referee's-eye view the harness is entitled to — is
  now the only reader, and a bout that has not finished will raise rather than
  leak a partial card even to the harness.

The ten-point-must arithmetic moved verbatim, together with the two logged
corrections that shaped it (rounds scored alone, effective offence not contact
counts). The referee did **not** move: counts, the three-knockdown rule and
stoppages are ring facts and stay in the engine — the judge witnesses the
knockdown's publicity, not its enforcement. Referee-profile consumption is
slice item 2 and touches the ring, not this module.

## The boundary, checked

`tools/uppercut_validate/judge_boundary.py` (wired into `cli all`, mirrored in
`tests/test_judge.py`) holds five relations over the two source files: the
Judge imports nothing; the Judge's AST contains no ring-state identifier; every
`witness()` call site in the engine passes a dict literal whose constant keys
and type come from the judge's published fact vocabulary; `sealed_cards()` is
called from `decision()` and nowhere else; and the judge object is never
aliased, passed onward, or reached into past its four public methods. Each
check is proven non-vacuous by a synthetic violation in the tests, in the same
discipline as the isolation scanner's violation sample.

Two boundary hardenings landed alongside, in the existing Rule 3 machinery:
`data/score_isolation_contract.json` now names `tools/uppercut_sim/policy.py`
as the real `cpu_fighter_logic` consumer and `tools/uppercut_sim/judge.py` as
the judge-subsystem holder, and the live repository scan in `cli isolation` is
**load-bearing** as of this change — it fails the run on a sealed-symbol
reference instead of printing an INFO count for a tree it never actually
scanned.

## The honest limit

The vocabulary check makes widening what the judge sees a *visible, reviewable
act* — a new key or fact type fails the validator until it is added to the
published vocabulary. It cannot prove the engine labels facts truthfully: code
that deliberately passed stamina as `"force"` would satisfy the shape. The
firewall's claim is auditability at two named call sites, not clairvoyance,
which is the same claim the CPU firewall makes and the same reason both keep
their non-vacuity samples.

## Equivalence evidence

The extraction is a refactor and was held to the refactor's standard: **byte
identity**. Every ordered profile pairing (6 stress profiles, 36 pairings) at
seeds 31 and 4242 — 72 bouts — was hashed (SHA-256 over the full event log,
`result()`, and `decision()`, sort-keyed JSON) at the pre-extraction commit
(`af087b0`) and again on the extracted tree:

    grand hash, before  b0c9ca2868315ad05fae69be00519927d1c1b84e434820ee2cbd2fa6e2d9ff75
    grand hash, after   b0c9ca2868315ad05fae69be00519927d1c1b84e434820ee2cbd2fa6e2d9ff75
    mismatching bouts   0 of 72

One design choice makes this exactness possible and is recorded in the judge's
own docstring: the judge witnesses the punch, not the log entry — the fact
carries the same unrounded force the engine computed at contact, where the
recorded event payload rounds to four decimals for serialization. Scoring from
the record instead would have changed card totals in the rounding tail and
made the refactor a rebalance. Judges score what they saw, not the broadcast
transcript.
